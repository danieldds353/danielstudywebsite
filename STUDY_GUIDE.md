# Using the midterm practice sets

The website now has separate **Practice Set 1** and **Practice Set 2** tabs, each with 100 questions and its own progress. The question-ID table below describes Set 1; Set 2 adds fresh scenarios across the same six chapters, with its own page references on every answer.

Read the textbook chapter, answer its practice questions without looking, and explain each missed distinction in your own words. Use the printed page references to return to the source. Then attempt the 100-question mixed mock. A high score on repeated identical questions may reflect familiarity; vary your reasoning by changing a condition in the scenario and predicting how the answer would change.

For a 90-minute, 100-question practice session, aim for a first pass in about 80 minutes and reserve about 10 minutes for flagged questions. This is a pacing recommendation, not an instructor requirement.

## Calculation sheet

- **NPV:** sum of each period’s net cash flow divided by `(1 + r)^t`. A time-zero outflow has `t = 0`. Match the discount rate to the period. Use the stated cash-flow timing.
- **ROI:** `(benefits − costs) / costs × 100%`. Use discounted totals when specified. Do not confuse benefits divided by costs with ROI.
- **Payback:** find when cumulative net cash flow recovers the investment. Check whether the question uses discounted or undiscounted flows. Do not assume within-year timing when only year-end flows are given.
- **Weighted score:** sum of `weight × score`; weights total 1 or 100%. Use the same scoring direction and units for all alternatives.
- **PERT:** `(optimistic + 4 × most likely + pessimistic) / 6`.
- **Simple effort-to-duration example:** 24 person-hours / 6 productive hours per day = 4 workdays. Adding workers does not always divide duration proportionally; work may not be divisible and coordination adds effort.
- **Critical path:** longest start-to-finish path under the network assumptions. It sets the earliest completion. More than one path may be critical.
- **Forward pass, zero-lag finish-to-start network:** `EF = ES + duration`; at a merge, `ES = max(predecessor EFs)`.
- **Backward pass, same assumptions:** `LS = LF − duration`; at a split, `LF = min(successor LSs)`.
- **Total float:** `LS − ES = LF − EF`. **Free float**, for the same simple relationships: earliest immediate successor ES minus current EF.
- Do not add every activity’s float together as if each could be used independently. Float can be shared along a path.

## High-value distinctions

- Business case = why the investment is worthwhile. Project charter = formal authorization. Team charter = working agreements.
- Project management plan = integrated management approach and baselines. A Gantt chart is only a schedule view.
- Requirements = needs/conditions. Scope statement = detailed scope and boundaries. WBS = hierarchy of project work. WBS dictionary = detail about its components. Activity list = work actions for scheduling.
- Scope baseline = approved scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary.
- Control Quality = correctness/verification. Validate Scope = formal acceptance. Control Scope = scope status and changes. Acceptance does not eliminate the need for closure.
- Corrective action realigns performance; preventive action reduces future negative consequences; defect repair addresses a nonconforming product.
- Systems analysis solves problems by examining components and alternatives; systems management considers business, organizational, and technological issues.
- A strong matrix gives the PM more authority than a balanced or weak matrix. “Matrix” alone does not imply identical authority in every organization.
- Process groups classify management processes; life-cycle phases organize stages of the project. They are not one-to-one equivalents.
- Sprint review inspects product results; retrospective improves how the team works; a daily Scrum provides short coordination. Product backlog and sprint backlog have different scopes.
- Outsourcing is about an external provider; offshoring is about another country.
- Fast tracking overlaps work; crashing seeks cost-effective schedule reduction. Shortening a noncritical task or only one of multiple critical paths may not shorten the project.

## Edition notes and internal inconsistencies

Use the ninth edition’s terminology for this bank; do not silently substitute a newer PMBOK framework.

- **Integration, pp. 152–153:** the enumerated list and Figure 4-1 show **seven** processes, including Manage Project Knowledge, although surrounding prose refers to six. The bank follows the seven-process list and Chapter 3 mapping.
- **Process mapping, pp. 91–92:** Estimate Activity Resources is under **resource management**, not schedule management. Older time-management materials may differ.
- **Talent triangle, p. 28:** this edition uses technical project management, leadership, and strategic and business management. The questions use these labels.
- **Scrum, pp. 131–134:** the chapter discusses product backlog, sprint backlog, and burndown chart as three artifacts. Its 2–4-week sprint examples and JWD story-point/hour conversion should be understood in the assigned textbook context, not treated as universal rules for every Scrum team. Do not assume a newer Scrum guide has exactly the same presentation.
- **Precedence relationships, p. 251:** nearby start-to-start prose reverses the predecessor/successor direction relative to the correct relationship shown in the figure. Start-to-start means the successor cannot start until the predecessor starts; it does not mean waiting for the predecessor to finish.
- Historical membership counts, software prices, salaries, and examples reflect the book’s publication era. They are not verified current facts. The bank emphasizes concepts rather than memorizing dated statistics.
